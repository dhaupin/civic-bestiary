# Candidate entries

Coined names that are liked but have no entry yet, and characters that have been
described but not named. The lexicon is the guide; this is the waiting room.

A candidate is not a record. Nothing here is loaded, built, or served - no entry
page, no README row, no card. When one ships, delete it from this file and let
git keep the argument.

## How a candidate is written

Each one carries the four things a record would need, because a name that cannot
survive them is not ready:

- **Name and reading** - the blend, the IPA, the line it has to land on.
- **Mechanism** - what the character does, in one sentence, with the loop it runs.
- **Seat** - the chapter it would file in, and the entries it would sit beside.
- **Failure mode** - the reason it might be a synonym, a person, or nothing. An
  empty failure mode means the candidate is ready and should be written.

## Spaghettist

**noun** · /spəˈɡɛt.ɪst/ · blend of "spaghetti" + "-ist"

The one who throws it all at the wall to see what sticks: policies announced
faster than they can be funded, accusations made in bulk, three plans in a week
and no way to tell which one was meant.

- **Call** - "We'll see what sticks."
- **Seat** - II, Bad-Faith Debate. Beside `Flood the zone`, the volume move
  performed by institutions, and `Ricochetoric`, volume performed by one person
  against one question.
- **Counter** - ask which one they believed *before* it stuck. The wall is a
  test, so the question is what the thrower was willing to claim while it was
  still unfalsified.
- **Failure mode** - it is a synonym for `Ricochetoric` unless the entry stays on
  the wall rather than the claims. The ricochetoric never owns a claim; the
  spaghettist owns all of them at once and none of them in particular. If the
  entry cannot hold that line, it should not ship.

**Status:** name approved, deliberately unused. Held for the version that is
about testing rather than arguing.

## Recollectomy

**noun** · /ˌriː.kəˈlɛk.tə.mi/ · blend of "recollection" + "-ectomy"

The memory version of the dodge: not "I was not told" but "I do not recall" - the
surgical removal of a briefing, performed live, under oath, without anaesthetic.

- **Call** - "I don't have any recollection of that."
- **Seat** - III, beside `Unawares`, which took the information half.
- **Counter** - produce the document he signed and ask him to read it aloud. A
  memory cannot be subpoenaed; a signature can.
- **Failure mode** - `Unawares` already covers the uninformedness dodge and
  shipped first. Recollectomy only earns an entry if it stays on *memory* - the
  witness who remembers every hour except the one that matters - because a second
  "I didn't know" move would read as a synonym.

**Status:** strong name, no entry. The distinction from `Unawares` is the whole
candidate: one is about being told, the other about remembering.
